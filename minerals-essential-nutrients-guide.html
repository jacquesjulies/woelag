Minerals and Essential Nutrients: What to Check on the Label

Minerals support many normal body functions, from nerve signalling and muscle function to oxygen transport. Yet the right choice is not simply the product with the biggest dose or the most complicated name.

Different minerals, different jobs

Magnesium is involved in muscle and nerve function; zinc supports immune function and wound healing; iron is needed to make haemoglobin. These are different nutrients with different requirements, and one cannot replace another.

Form and elemental amount

Labels may list a compound such as magnesium bisglycinate or zinc citrate. Check how much of the actual mineral—often called elemental magnesium or elemental zinc—is supplied per serving. The compound weight alone may not tell you the nutrient amount.

Food sources and individual needs

Beans, nuts, seeds, whole grains, vegetables and other foods contribute minerals; animal foods can also supply nutrients such as iron and zinc. Needs vary and a suspected deficiency may require clinical assessment rather than guesswork.

When more can be too much

Excess iron can be dangerous and should not be taken routinely without a reason. Too much zinc over time can reduce copper status. Magnesium supplements can cause diarrhoea and may be unsuitable in significant kidney disease. More is not automatically more effective.

Safety notes

Mineral supplements can affect how some medicines are absorbed, including certain antibiotics and thyroid medicines. Ask a pharmacist or healthcare professional about timing and suitability if you take medication, have kidney disease or another chronic condition.

A practical next step

Use this guide as a starting point, then read the product label carefully. Product availability, formulations and prices can change, so verify details with the retailer before buying.
