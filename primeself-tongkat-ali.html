PrimeSelf Tongkat Ali: Traditional Herb, Testosterone and Energy

Known in Southeast Asian herbal traditions, tongkat ali has attracted modern interest for its possible links with male hormonal health, mood and physical performance. What does the research actually suggest, and what should you know about this particular supplement?

At a glance: PrimeSelf Tongkat Ali is a plant-extract supplement standardized to 2% eurycomanone. Early human research is promising for some outcomes, particularly testosterone in certain adult men, but the evidence base is still small and does not guarantee an effect for every person.

What is tongkat ali?

Tongkat ali, also known by its botanical name Eurycoma longifolia, is a plant native to parts of Southeast Asia. Its roots have a long history of traditional use, including in preparations associated with vitality and male wellbeing. Modern supplements use root extracts, which can differ in strength and composition depending on how they are prepared.

One compound often used to describe extract strength is eurycomanone. PrimeSelf lists its product as standardized to 2% eurycomanone, giving buyers a more specific description of the extract than a label that simply says “tongkat ali.” Standardization alone, however, does not prove a particular health outcome.

Why are people interested in it?

Tongkat ali is commonly marketed around male vitality, stress resilience, energy, testosterone and exercise performance. Researchers have investigated some of these questions, but the findings vary by study, extract, participant group and outcome measured.

  • Testosterone: Some clinical studies have reported increases in total testosterone, particularly in men whose levels were low at the start. That does not mean everyone who takes tongkat ali will experience a rise.
  • Stress and mood: A few small studies have explored stress-related measures and mood. More robust research is needed before dependable effects can be promised.
  • Exercise performance and recovery: These are popular reasons people consider the herb, but evidence for consistent improvements in strength, endurance or recovery remains limited.

What does the scientific research say?

A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis examined clinical trials of Eurycoma longifolia and testosterone in adult men. Nine studies were included in the review, with five contributing to the meta-analysis. The authors reported a possible benefit for total testosterone, while also noting that more research is needed before the herb can be confidently recommended in clinical practice.

This is an encouraging research signal, not a guarantee and not a substitute for investigating symptoms or a suspected hormone problem. Testosterone naturally varies, and concerns about persistently low levels need proper clinical assessment and appropriately timed laboratory testing. Supplements should not be used to self-diagnose a hormone condition.

Building vitality beyond a supplement

Energy, mood and exercise performance are influenced by many factors: sleep, nutrition, stress, physical activity and overall health. A supplement cannot compensate for ongoing sleep problems, an unbalanced diet or an untreated medical issue. If fatigue, low mood, reduced libido or other changes persist, discussing them with a healthcare professional can help identify the cause rather than guessing at it.
