Phytoceutics Phytofemme PCOSupport: Ingredients, Evidence and Safety

Inositol supplements are often discussed in relation to polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), a complex hormonal and metabolic condition. Here is a closer look at this multi-ingredient powder, what research suggests, and where the evidence is still uncertain.

Product format: 300 g powder · Label serving: 10 g · Manufacturer says for adults aged 18 and older

View product →

The short version: Phytofemme PCOSupport combines myo-inositol and D-chiro-inositol with minerals, vitamins, alpha-lipoic acid and CoQ10. Inositol may be considered by some adults with PCOS, but international guidance describes the clinical benefits as limited and says evidence is not strong enough to recommend a specific type, dose or combination. It is not a proven cure for PCOS or a guaranteed fertility treatment.

What is Phytofemme PCOSupport?

Phytoceutics markets this powder as nutritional support for female reproductive health, hormonal balance and metabolic function. Those are intended uses, not proof that the finished product treats PCOS, restores ovulation, improves fertility or prevents diabetes. PCOS varies from person to person, and a suitable care plan depends on symptoms, health history and personal goals.

What's in the daily serving?

The retailer information supplied for this guide lists a 10 g serving mixed with 200 ml water. It describes a 300 g carton with about 30 servings. Listed ingredients include:

  • Myo-inositol — 4,000 mg and D-chiro-inositol — 100 mg, a 40:1 ratio within the inositol complex.
  • Calcium — 500 mg elemental, supplied as calcium carbonate.
  • Magnesium — 250 mg elemental, supplied as magnesium bisglycinate.
  • Alpha-lipoic acid — 400 mg, and coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10).
  • Zinc — 25 mg elemental, supplied as zinc oxide.
  • Folate — 500 micrograms, listed as L-5-methyltetrahydrofolate.
  • Chromium — 30 micrograms elemental, alongside chromium polynicotinate.
  • Vitamin K2 — 45 micrograms, vitamin D3 — 25 micrograms, and vitamin B12 — 25 micrograms.
  • Stevia extract, a small amount of glucose, citric acid, isomaltooligosaccharide, orange flavouring, silicon dioxide and sodium chloride.
Check the current label before relying on these figures. The retailer information supplied for this article lists CoQ10 as 31 mg and chromium polynicotinate as 200 micrograms, while the manufacturer's current product page lists CoQ10 as 100 mg and chromium polynicotinate as 250 micrograms (providing 30 micrograms elemental chromium). Because these details differ, confirm the container label or ask the manufacturer before purchase or use.

What does research say about inositol and PCOS?

Inositol compounds are involved in cell signalling, including pathways related to insulin action. Researchers have studied myo-inositol and D-chiro-inositol in people with PCOS, including possible effects on some metabolic measures and reproductive markers. But results across studies vary, and evidence for one ingredient or study formulation does not prove that this particular multi-ingredient product will have the same effect.

The 2023 International Evidence-based Guideline for PCOS says inositol could be considered according to an adult's preferences and values, with potential limited harm and possible improvement in some metabolic measures. It also notes limited clinical benefits for ovulation, excess hair growth and weight. The guideline says evidence quality is not sufficient to recommend a particular type, dose or combination. For infertility, it considers inositol an experimental therapy because the benefits and risks remain too uncertain to recommend it as a fertility treatment.

What about blood glucose, energy and emotional wellbeing?

Chromium and magnesium have recognised roles in normal nutrient metabolism, while vitamin B12 and folate are important nutrients. That does not mean a supplement reliably improves blood glucose, fatigue or mood for everyone. Effects depend on factors such as nutritional status, health conditions and other treatments. A nutrient's normal biological role should not be confused with evidence that this exact formula produces a meaningful health outcome.

PCOS can be associated with insulin resistance and a higher risk of metabolic problems, but supplements should not replace appropriate screening, follow-up, nutrition support or medication when prescribed. Persistent fatigue or changes in mood also deserve attention rather than being automatically attributed to PCOS.

Does the 40:1 ratio guarantee results?

A 40:1 myo-inositol to D-chiro-inositol ratio is used in some products and research protocols. However, the presence of a ratio that has been studied does not prove that every product using it is clinically effective. The international guideline does not currently endorse a specific inositol type, ratio, dose or combination for PCOS because the evidence is not strong enough.

Safety and practical considerations

  • The manufacturer states that this product is intended for adults aged 18 and older. It is not intended for children or teenagers.
  • Ask a qualified healthcare professional before use if pregnant or breastfeeding, taking medicines, or living with a medical condition.
  • If you take medicine that affects blood glucose, discuss the formula with your clinician or pharmacist before using it because it contains ingredients marketed for metabolic support.
  • The listed 25 mg of elemental zinc per serving is a meaningful supplemental amount. Check other supplements you take to avoid unintentionally stacking zinc or other nutrients.
  • Vitamin K2 can interact with warfarin and related anticoagulant treatment. Anyone using these medicines should not start or change a vitamin K-containing supplement without medical advice.
  • The product contains a sweetener and a small amount of glucose; the supplied retailer listing says it is not sugar-free.
  • Do not exceed the label directions, and stop and seek advice if you experience a concerning reaction.
PCOS symptoms deserve proper assessment. Irregular or absent periods, troublesome acne or excess facial hair, unexpected changes in symptoms, or difficulty conceiving can have different causes. A clinician can help confirm the diagnosis and discuss evidence-based options. Do not stop prescribed treatment or delay care in favour of a supplement.

Our take

Phytofemme PCOSupport offers a broad combination of inositols and nutrients in one powder, but the strongest independent guidance still describes uncertainty about the clinical benefits of inositol for PCOS, particularly fertility outcomes. If an adult is considering it, a useful next step is to discuss their goals, medicines and existing supplements with a qualified healthcare professional—and verify the label because some online ingredient amounts appear inconsistent.
