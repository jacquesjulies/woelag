New Leaf Tribulus Tablets: Ingredients, Evidence and Safety

Tribulus terrestris and ashwagandha are often combined in supplements marketed for vitality. Before considering any product, it helps to separate traditional use and marketing language from what good-quality research has actually established.

The short answer: This product combines concentrated Tribulus terrestris extract with ashwagandha root extract. Although both herbs are used in wellness products, evidence that tribulus reliably increases testosterone, muscle strength or athletic performance is limited and inconsistent. Research on ashwagandha suggests possible effects on stress in some adults, but findings depend on the preparation and study, and it is not suitable for everyone.

What does the evidence say about tribulus?

Tribulus terrestris has a history of traditional use and is widely marketed for libido, vitality and sports performance. However, human studies have not consistently shown that it increases testosterone or reliably improves strength and athletic performance. Evidence for sexual wellbeing is also limited, and a result from one small study cannot guarantee an effect for an individual.

That means claims about hormone balance, muscle gains or improved sexual function should be treated as marketing claims rather than guaranteed outcomes. If you have persistent changes in energy, mood, exercise capacity or sexual health, a healthcare professional can help investigate possible causes.

What about ashwagandha?

Ashwagandha is used in traditional herbal practices and has been studied for stress and sleep. Some small trials suggest possible benefits for certain adults, but studies vary in product type, dose, duration and quality. It should not be presented as a proven treatment for anxiety, a hormonal condition or any other medical diagnosis.

Ashwagandha may cause side effects and has been associated with rare cases of liver injury. It may also affect thyroid function and interact with medicines, including some medicines for blood pressure, diabetes, seizures, immune suppression or sedation. Ask a clinician or pharmacist before using it if you take medication or have a health condition.

Who should take extra care?

  • Anyone who is pregnant or breastfeeding should not use this product without specific professional advice.
  • People taking prescription medicines or being monitored for a medical condition should consult a GP or pharmacist first.
  • People with thyroid or liver conditions should seek medical advice before considering ashwagandha.
  • Do not combine multiple herbal products for similar goals without checking their ingredients and possible interactions.

Supplements are not a substitute for a balanced diet, appropriate training, sleep or medical care. A healthcare professional can help determine whether a supplement is appropriate for your circumstances.
