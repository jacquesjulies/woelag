Vitamins A to K: A Simple Guide to Choosing Supplements

Vitamins help the body carry out essential processes, but a supplement is not automatically better simply because it contains more nutrients. The best starting point is understanding the job each vitamin does and checking whether a product suits your needs.

Fat-soluble and water-soluble vitamins

Vitamins A, D, E and K are fat-soluble. Vitamins C and the B-group vitamins are water-soluble. These broad groups differ in how the body absorbs and stores them; fat-soluble vitamins can accumulate, so high-dose use deserves particular care.

Why each label matters

Check the amount per serving, the percentage of the reference intake, the form of each nutrient, the serving size and whether the product duplicates nutrients from other supplements. A multivitamin plus separate high-dose products can push total intake higher than intended.

Food remains important

A varied diet provides vitamins alongside fibre, protein, minerals and other compounds that a pill cannot fully reproduce. Supplements can be useful in specific circumstances, but needs depend on age, diet, health, pregnancy status and medicines.

Beware of broad claims

A vitamin contributes to normal body functions; that does not mean taking extra will improve energy, immunity or performance when you already get enough. Claims that a supplement treats a disease need stronger evidence than a list of nutrient functions.

Safety notes

High doses of vitamin A can be harmful and are especially important to discuss during pregnancy. Vitamin K can interfere with warfarin management. Vitamin D and other nutrients can also be harmful in excess. Ask a healthcare professional before using high-dose products or combining several supplements, particularly if you take chronic medication.

A practical next step

Use this guide as a starting point, then read the product label carefully. Product availability, formulations and prices can change, so verify details with the retailer before buying.
