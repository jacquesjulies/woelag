Metagenics GlutaClear: Antioxidants, Ingredients and Evidence

An evidence-aware look at Metagenics GlutaClear, its glutathione-support ingredients, antioxidant biology, limitations and safety considerations.

Product guide · Antioxidants and cellular health · Product details checked against the manufacturer and retailer listings; prices and formulations can change.

Oxidative stress is part of normal biology, but excessive oxidative damage can contribute to cell and tissue injury. The body uses several antioxidant systems to keep this process in balance. One is glutathione, a molecule made by the body from amino-acid building blocks.

What is GlutaClear?

Metagenics GlutaClear is a complementary-medicine supplement marketed to support glutathione production and antioxidant defence. The South African product listing says one tablet daily with food, or as directed by a healthcare practitioner, and states it is not recommended for children under 18. It also notes that the product has not been evaluated or verified by SAHPRA for its quality, safety or intended use. Read the manufacturer's product information.

What's in the formula?

  • N-acetyl-L-cysteine (NAC), 375 mg: provides cysteine, one of the building blocks used to make glutathione.
  • Vitamin C, 250 mg: an essential nutrient that also functions as an antioxidant.
  • Niacinamide (vitamin B3), 375 mg: a form of vitamin B3 involved in normal energy metabolism. This is a substantial dose compared with everyday nutritional requirements.
  • Selenium, 37.5 micrograms: a trace mineral required for certain antioxidant enzymes.
  • Broccoli seed extract, 19.25 mg: listed as providing glucoraphanin, a plant compound associated with pathways involved in cellular responses.

These amounts are taken from the South African manufacturer's listing for one tablet. Always check the current container and leaflet, since labels or product versions can change. View the retailer's current listing.

What does the antioxidant science tell us?

Glutathione has a well-established role in the body's antioxidant and cellular processes. That does not automatically mean that taking a supplement will prevent disease, slow ageing, improve energy or produce a noticeable benefit in a generally healthy person. The strength of evidence depends on the outcome studied, the population and the formulation.

GlutaClear's ingredients have biological roles, but the product page's claims should not be read as proof that this particular supplement treats a medical condition or delivers a specific clinical outcome. Supplements also cannot replace a varied diet, medical care or treatment prescribed for a diagnosed condition.

Safety and practical considerations

Please note: This product is not recommended for anyone under 18. Adults who are pregnant or breastfeeding, take medication, have a health condition, or are considering a high-dose supplement should discuss it with a qualified healthcare professional first. The niacinamide dose is high relative to normal dietary requirements, and NAC and other ingredients may not be suitable for everyone.
  • Do not combine several high-dose products without checking the total intake of overlapping nutrients.
  • Follow the current product label rather than advice from social media or another country's formulation.
  • Seek medical advice for persistent symptoms rather than trying to “detox” or treat them with a supplement.
