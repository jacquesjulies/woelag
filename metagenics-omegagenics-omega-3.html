Omega-3 Fatty Acids: Getting to Know EPA and DHA

Some nutrients work quietly in the background. Omega-3 fats are part of that story: here’s what EPA and DHA do, what this Metagenics fish-oil product provides, and what to check before choosing a supplement.

Omega-3s are not a miracle shortcut. They are essential fats with important roles in the body, and the most useful starting point is understanding the difference between food sources, the types of omega-3, and the claims a supplement can reasonably make.

What are omega-3 fatty acids?

Omega-3 is a family of fats. ALA is found in foods such as flaxseed, chia seeds and walnuts. EPA and DHA are found mainly in oily fish and seafood. The body can convert some ALA into EPA and DHA, but the conversion is limited. A varied eating pattern that includes appropriate omega-3 food sources is a sensible foundation for general nutrition.

Why do EPA and DHA matter?

  • Heart health: EPA and DHA contribute to normal body functions; prescription-strength omega-3 medicines may be used for specific blood-triglyceride conditions under medical supervision.
  • Brain and eyes: DHA is an important structural component of the brain and retina.
  • Inflammation: These fats are involved in processes that regulate inflammation, but this does not mean a supplement treats every inflammatory condition.

Research outcomes vary by person, dose, health status and reason for use. Taking fish oil should not be presented as a way to clear blocked arteries, prevent a heart attack, or replace prescribed treatment.

Food first, supplement second

For many people, food is a practical way to obtain nutrients. Oily fish such as sardines, pilchards, salmon and mackerel provide EPA and DHA; plant foods such as chia, flaxseed and walnuts provide ALA. Whether a supplement is appropriate depends on diet, age, health needs and medicines—not simply on a product’s marketing claims.

Safety checks before use

  • Check the label for the exact amount of EPA and DHA, not only the total fish-oil weight.
  • Fish oil may be unsuitable for people with fish allergies.
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before using it if you take regular medication—especially blood-thinning medicines—or have a medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, or are preparing for surgery.
  • Do not stop or change prescribed medicines because of a supplement.
