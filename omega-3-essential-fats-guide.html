Omega-3 and Essential Fats: What to Know Before You Buy

Some nutrients deserve a closer look—not because one capsule can transform your health, but because understanding what your body needs can make everyday choices clearer. Omega-3 fats are essential fats that play important roles in normal body function.

What are omega-3 fats?

The main omega-3 fats discussed in nutrition are ALA, EPA and DHA. ALA is found in foods such as flaxseed, chia seeds and walnuts. EPA and DHA are found in oily fish and some algae-based products. The body can convert some ALA to EPA and DHA, but conversion is limited.

What does the evidence say?

EPA and DHA are components of cell membranes and are involved in normal cardiovascular and other body functions. Eating fish as part of a balanced diet can fit within a heart-healthy eating pattern. Supplement trials do not show that every person benefits from taking fish oil, and supplements should not be described as a way to prevent or cure disease.

How to compare labels

Look beyond the large number on the front of a bottle. Check the amount of EPA and DHA per serving, serving size, source (fish or algae), expiry date, storage advice and quality/testing information. Total fish oil is not the same as the combined EPA and DHA amount.

Food first, when practical

Consider oily fish and plant sources such as chia, flaxseed and walnuts within a varied diet. An algae-based supplement may be an option for people who avoid fish, but individual needs vary.

Safety notes

Omega-3 supplements can cause digestive effects and may interact with medicines that affect blood clotting, particularly at higher doses. Ask a healthcare professional before starting one if you take regular medication, have a bleeding disorder, are preparing for surgery or have a chronic condition.

A practical next step

Use this guide as a starting point, then read the product label carefully. Product availability, formulations and prices can change, so verify details with the retailer before buying.
