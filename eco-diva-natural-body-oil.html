What is in the formula?

The retailer lists sweet almond, sunflower, avocado, sesame, marula and jojoba oils, alongside grapefruit peel oil, vitamin E (tocopherol), vanilla extract and bergamot peel oil. Plant oils can help soften skin and reduce moisture loss by leaving an emollient layer on its surface. Vitamin E is commonly used as an antioxidant ingredient in skin-care formulas.

The feel and scent of a body oil are personal. This formula includes several nut- or seed-derived oils and fragrant citrus oils, so anyone with known allergies or sensitive skin should check the complete ingredient list carefully.

How the retailer says to use it

  • Apply to damp skin during or after a bath or shower.
  • Spread a small amount evenly and allow time for it to absorb before dressing.
  • The retailer also lists it as a possible shaving oil.

Use only as directed on the current packaging and keep it for external use. Oils can make bath or shower surfaces slippery, so take care.

What should you realistically expect?

A body oil can make skin feel softer and more comfortable by helping lubricate the skin and slow moisture loss. That is different from proving that a product prevents stretch marks, repairs scars, balances skin pH or provides dependable sun protection. We could not verify independent clinical evidence showing that this particular product delivers those specific outcomes.

Before you buy

  • Check the current ingredient list and any allergy warnings.
  • Try a small amount on a limited area first if you are unsure how your skin will react.
  • Do not use it on irritated or broken skin unless the label says it is suitable.
  • Do not treat a body oil as a replacement for tested sunscreen.
