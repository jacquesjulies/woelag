Vitamin K2 MK-7 and Artery Health: What Does the Science Say?

It is an intriguing question: can a nutrient involved in activating certain proteins also influence the way calcium builds up in blood vessels? Researchers are studying that question. Here is what we know—and what we cannot yet promise.

The short answer: Vitamin K is needed to activate matrix Gla protein (MGP), a protein involved in regulating abnormal calcification in blood vessels. But clinical trials have not consistently shown that K2 supplements stop artery calcification, prevent heart attacks, or clear existing blockages. K2 is not a proven treatment for clogged arteries.

Why researchers are interested in vitamin K2

Vitamin K is a fat-soluble nutrient that helps activate proteins involved in normal blood clotting and bone metabolism. One vitamin K-dependent protein, matrix Gla protein (MGP), is found in vascular tissue and is studied because of its role in regulating calcification. This gives researchers a biological reason to investigate whether vitamin K status matters for artery health.

That mechanism is a research lead, not proof that taking a supplement removes plaque or opens a narrowed artery. Arterial calcification is also not the same thing as every form of plaque or atherosclerosis, and these terms should not be used interchangeably.

What do human studies show?

A 2023 systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 14 randomised controlled trials involving 1,533 participants. It reported a modest slowing of coronary artery calcification scores across the included vitamin K trials, alongside improvements in a biochemical marker of vitamin K status. The authors nevertheless called for better-designed trials to confirm the clinical value. The analysis included vitamin K interventions and does not establish that this specific K2 product clears arteries.

A separate systematic review of controlled trials found that vitamin K supplementation did not consistently prevent progression of vascular calcification, atherosclerosis or arterial stiffness. A 2025 review discussing K2 and coronary calcification also concluded that clinical evidence supporting a protective effect in patients remains lacking.

Taken together, the evidence supports continued research—not a claim that K2 reverses clogged arteries or reliably prevents cardiovascular disease.

Could K2 support cardiovascular health?

There is a plausible biological pathway, and some studies report encouraging changes in markers or calcification measures. But those findings are not the same as proving fewer heart attacks, strokes or deaths. The US National Institutes of Health Office of Dietary Supplements says more research is needed to determine whether vitamin K supplements might help prevent heart disease.

For cardiovascular risk, do not replace prescribed medicines or medical follow-up with supplements. A clinician can assess blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes risk, smoking exposure and other factors that have established roles in cardiovascular prevention.

Safety matters

Vitamin K can interact seriously with warfarin and similar vitamin K-antagonist medicines. People taking these medicines should not start, stop or change vitamin K supplements without guidance from their prescribing healthcare professional. Ask a pharmacist or clinician about suitability if you take regular medication or have a medical condition.

Our takeaway

Vitamin K2 is scientifically interesting because it helps activate proteins involved in bone metabolism and vascular biology. However, it is not established as a way to clean clogged arteries, reverse plaque or prevent cardiovascular events. The most useful product decision starts with accurate information, realistic expectations and professional advice when needed.
